Rook

The classic card game played with a Rook deck using Dixon family rules.

Rook needs four players, each on their own device.

How to Play

The deck, bidding, tricks, scoring, playing online

The Deck

The deck has 57 cards: 1 through 14 in Black, Red, Green and Yellow, plus the Rook. Partners sit across from each other, so play alternates between teams.

Deal & Bid

  1. Everyone gets 13 cards and 5 go face down in the middle as the widow. If anyone is dealt a hand with no point cards, the cards are reshuffled.
  2. Starting left of the dealer, each player bids (100 to 180, in steps of 5) or passes. Once you pass you're out of the bidding. If the first three players pass, the last player is stuck with 100.
  3. The winner can raise their own bid before seeing the widow.
  4. The widow is turned face up for everyone. Once all four players click Got It, the bid winner picks it up, buries any 5 cards, and names trump.

Playing Tricks

  1. The bid winner leads the first trick. Whoever wins a trick leads the next one.
  2. You must follow the suit that was led. If you can't, you must play trump if you have any. Otherwise play anything.
  3. The Rook is always trump and is the highest card in the game. 1s are high (above 14).
  4. The highest trump wins the trick. With no trump played, the highest card of the led suit wins.

Scoring

  • Point cards: Rook 20, each 1 is 15, each 10 and 14 is 10, each 5 is 5. That's 180 points per hand.
  • The team that takes the last trick also gets the points buried in the widow.
  • The bidding team scores their bid if they took at least that many points, and loses their bid if they didn't. The other team scores whatever points they took.
  • First team to 500 (and ahead) wins.
  • When the game ends, anyone can download it as a CSV for a spreadsheet: Hand by Hand (one row per hand), Trick by Trick (one row per trick), or, after a few rounds of Play Again, All Games from the session. The host can also export the game so far from the Score Sheet at any time.

Playing Online

Enter your name, click Host a Game, and send the link to three friends. The host arranges the teams and starts the game. Your browsers connect directly to each other, so the host needs to keep their tab open until the game is done. If anyone else drops, they can rejoin with the same link and the same name.

Voice Chat

Want to talk while you play? Click Join Voice and allow the microphone. It's opt in: nobody's mic turns on until they click it. You'll hear everyone else who joined, a 🎙️ shows who's in, and a player's seat glows green while they talk. Headphones help keep echo down. Like the game, the audio goes straight between your browsers.

Game Options

The host can turn these on or off at any time. Both start off.

  • Live scores shows how many points each team has taken during the hand, and how far the bidding team is from making their bid.
  • Game log lets everyone look back at every trick: who led, what each player played, and who took it. Handy when someone is learning the game.
